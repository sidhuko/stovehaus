# Stovehaus — content strategy, year one

Written against [BRAND.md](BRAND.md). Voice rule 2 governs everything here:
**every claim carries a number or a name, or it gets cut.** That single rule
disqualifies most of the content this category publishes, and it is the reason
the plan below looks narrower than a normal editorial calendar.

---

## The situation, stated plainly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Stage** | Pre-launch. No dealers signed, no customers, no calls to mine. |
| **Offer** | Three heater lines plus complete sauna rooms, quoted to the drawing. |
| **Evidence on hand** | None tested. No certification, no measured specs, no product or shop photography. |
| **Audience** | Dealers/installers first, end buyers once there are figures and photographs. |
| **Languages** | English and es-MX, every piece, at full parity. |
| **Capacity** | 2 pieces a month. |

### The capacity number is really half what it looks like

Spanish here has to be **written** to the voice rules, not translated — that is
the standard the site already holds. So a bilingual pair is one piece of work,
not two. **The real budget is ~12 original pieces in year one**, which is why
this plan has no experimental slice: the usual 60/30/10 split becomes roughly
**7 searchable / 5 shareable / 0 experimental**. At twelve pieces a year, every
one has to be a keeper.

### Three things the research changed

**1. Certification is not a content topic. It is the business.**
UL 1482 is what US insurers require on a solid-fuel room heater. An uncertified
wood burner can fail inspection, and may leave a homeowner uncovered if it
causes a fire. No serious dealer will stock an unlisted stove, and no installer
will hang their licence on one. Everything below is downstream of this.

**The highest-leverage marketing action available to Stovehaus is not a blog
post. It is getting listed.** Content cannot route around that.

**2. The competitor set is consolidating, and Stovehaus is on the same side of
it.** Harvia owns Almost Heaven, and Harvia's North American offer is heaters
and controls sold *into* other people's rooms.

An earlier draft of this plan treated that as a wedge — pitch room builders a
heater supplier that is not their competitor's parent. **Stovehaus builds rooms
too, so that pitch is not available.** Room builders are competitors here, not
prospects, and the distribution section below has been corrected accordingly.

What replaces it is a different and arguably better argument for a *dealer*:
Stovehaus supplies the stove and the room, so a dealer can quote a whole job
rather than selling a stove and handing the room to somebody else. That is a
margin argument, which is the one dealers actually respond to.

**3. The stats-roundup play is already taken — by a retailer.**
Topture ranks for "sauna industry statistics". Curated stat pages earn roughly
4.25× their page share in backlinks, so it is the strongest link format there
is, but the broad version is occupied. A narrower one — wood-fired only, or
North American dealer economics — is still open. It is on the list, ranked last,
because it is cheap rather than because it is important.

## What content cannot fix, and should not pretend to

Three blockers gate most of the calendar. Naming them is the strategy, because
each one converts a blocked piece into a publishable one the moment it clears:

| Blocker | Unblocks |
|---|---|
| **No listing** | Clearances, the install guide, any dealer close |
| **No tested figures** | Every spec page, the sizing chart, the comparison content |
| **No photography** | The entire craft pillar, all of Instagram, half the homepage |

There is a fourth, and it is the cheapest to fix: **the quote form posts
nowhere.** Until it does, every piece of distribution below leaks. There is no
owned channel — no list, no capture, nothing that survives a platform change.
**Fix this before publishing anything.** It is an afternoon of work and it is
worth more than the first six months of the calendar.

## Content pillars

Three, not five. Twelve pieces across three pillars is four each — already thin.
A fourth pillar would make all of them decorative.

### Pillar 1 — Landing it legally

Certification, clearances, flue standards, import. The dealer's and installer's
first question and their last objection. Stovehaus will acquire this knowledge
because it has no choice, so writing it down is nearly free.

Nobody in this category publishes an honest, dated certification position.
Everyone is vague, because vagueness is comfortable. The guidelines already
prescribe the opposite: *"CSA listing in progress, expected Q2 beats silence,
and beats an implication that turns out to be wrong."*

### Pillar 2 — Say the number

Sizing, stone mass, output, room volume. The searchable foundation and the
pillar that carries both audiences. This is where the brand's own idea — "say
the number" — becomes content rather than a slogan.

There is a real, teachable error here that Stovehaus made itself: the 0002 event
stand printed the Versa 13's room capacity as **6–13 m²** when the rating is
room **volume**, 6–13 m³. That is the most common sizing mistake in the
category, and having made it publicly is what makes it credible to write about.

### Pillar 3 — Built heavy

The shop, the steel, the crew, the fire test. Message 01 and 02. The only pillar
no competitor can copy, and the only one gated entirely on photography.

### The two that are not blog posts

**#1 — the certification log is a permanent page, not an article.** A dated
table: line, standard, lab, status, expected date, last updated. Every entry is
a status and a date, which satisfies voice rule 2 even while the answer is "not
yet". It is the first link to send any dealer who asks, it is the page that
stops the question being asked by email, and it is the single most
differentiating thing Stovehaus could publish this year. Update it monthly
whether or not anything changed — "no change since March" is information.

**#2 — the sizing calculator is the flagship.** Interactive tools earn ~1.38×
their page share in links, it is the highest-intent moment in the category, and
it is almost free to localise because it is mostly numbers. It is also the
purest expression of the brand idea. Build it once, then let it carry pieces 3,
6, 9 and 10 as its written surround. Critically: **it should recommend a room
volume and an output band, not a Versa** — a calculator that only ever answers
"buy ours" gets linked by nobody.

### Dealer economics is not a blog post either

Margin, freight from Saltillo, USMCA treatment, HS 7321.81, lead times, parts
held on the continent. It is the most persuasive material Stovehaus has, and it
belongs in **sales enablement, not the blog** — a one-pager sent to a named
list. The addressable audience is dozens of companies, not a search market. Do
not spend scarce publishing capacity broadcasting at forty people you could
email. (See the `sales-enablement` skill for the format.)

## Sequencing

Twelve pieces, one a month, in the order the blockers clear.

### Phase 1 — months 1–6, dealer-first

Nothing here needs a tested figure or a photograph. All of it is publishable
now.

1. Certification & listing log *(page — ship first, before any post)*
2. Room volume, not floor area
3. Sizing calculator *(flagship — allow two months)*
4. What a UL 1482 listing actually means
5. How much stone a heater needs
6. Importing a wood burner into the US and Canada

### Phase 2 — months 7–12, end-buyer, as blockers clear

7. What we measured when we fired the first Versa — *the credibility moment.*
   The day the provisional markers come off the site is the single best content
   day Stovehaus will have. Plan it as a launch, not a post.
8. Clearances: what an installer needs to quote
9. The Versa sizing chart
10. Wood vs electric, honestly
11. How a firebox gets built
12. Wood-fired sauna, by the numbers

**If testing slips**, do not backfill with softer pieces. Hold the slot and
publish nothing. A brand whose entire argument is "we publish a figure once it
has been measured" cannot fill a gap with an adjective — it would cost more than
a quiet month does.

## On the Spanish half

Full parity is the instruction and this plan honours it, but the value is not
symmetrical and the reporting should not pretend otherwise.

- **Spanish will not earn search traffic in this category.** The buying market
  is the US and Canada; the Mexican and LatAm wood-fired sauna market is
  nascent. Measuring the Spanish tree on sessions will make it look like a
  failure when it is doing a different job.
- **Measure it on what it actually does:** credibility with Mexican trade,
  suppliers and press; recruitment; and brand consistency — the same argument
  that made the site bilingual.
- **Pillar 3 is the one that genuinely earns its Spanish version.** The shop,
  the crew, the fabrication: that is a Mexican story with a Mexican audience.
  If budget ever tightens, cut Spanish on Pillar 1 before Pillar 3.
- **Hire a native es-MX writer, do not translate.** The Spanish voice rules are
  in [`src/i18n/es.ts`](src/i18n/es.ts); brief from there. A translated piece
  will read as translated, and this brand's whole claim is that it does not
  oversell.

## Distribution — borrowed, rented, owned

Pre-launch with no audience, so it is borrowed-first by necessity.

**Owned (build it first — this does not exist yet)**
Wire the quote form. Add a dealer list opt-in. Without these, everything below
is pouring water into a bucket with no bottom.

**Borrowed (where the first thousand readers come from)**
- **Spa dealers and installers, not room builders.** Room builders are
  competitors now that Stovehaus builds rooms, so co-marketing there is off.
  The reachable list is spa and hearth retailers, pool and spa installers, and
  the trade bodies they belong to — people who want a whole job to sell rather
  than a category to compete in.
- **r/sauna.** Large, knowledgeable, and openly hostile to marketing. The only
  admission ticket is being genuinely useful — the calculator and the m³ piece
  qualify; a product post does not. Participate as a fabricator answering
  technical questions, never as a brand.
- **Sauna podcasts and the trade.** Stovehaus already does events — the 0001
  flyer and 0002 stand prove there is a room to be in. A manufacturer who will
  talk openly about certification is an unusual guest.

**Rented**
Per guidelines p.12, already decided: **LinkedIn carries the dealer story**
(lead times, capacity, listings, freight), **Instagram carries the end-buyer
story** (shop, fire, install, crate). Instagram is dead until photography
exists — do not open the account early and starve it.

**Atomise at creation, not after.** Write subheads that stand alone as posts,
pull the citable one-liners while drafting. The calculator alone should yield a
sizing infographic, three LinkedIn posts and the spine of the m³ article.

## What has to be built

The site has no blog, and adding one costs more here than usual because every
route exists twice. Scope before committing:

- `/blog/` and `/es/blog/`, with the same slug discipline as the rest of the
  site so the language switcher keeps working
- Content collections for posts, with the `Dict` pattern extended or a
  per-post frontmatter locale
- hreflang and route-parity — the existing audit already checks this and will
  fail the build on a post published in only one language, which is the correct
  behaviour and worth keeping

**Do not build this for piece #1.** The certification log is a page, not a post,
and the calculator is a page too. Both ship on the current architecture. Build
the blog when there is a third post to justify it — around month 4.

## How to tell if this is working

Vanity metrics will look bad for two quarters, and should. Track instead:

| Phase 1 (dealer) | Phase 2 (end buyer) |
|---|---|
| Dealer enquiries naming a piece of content | Organic sessions to Pillar 2 |
| Certification log views + repeat views | Calculator completions |
| Room builders in conversation | Enquiries with a room volume already stated |
| Backlinks from trade and builder sites | Instagram saves on shop content |

The one number that matters most in year one is not on either list: **whether
the listing lands.** Content can carry the company to the moment a dealer asks
"is it certified", and no further.
